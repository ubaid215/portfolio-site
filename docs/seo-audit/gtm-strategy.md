# GTM strategy: turning the portfolio into an acquisition asset

**Prepared:** 17 October 2026
**Situation:** solo full-stack developer, 4–6 hours/day available, near-zero budget, needs income soon, longer-term direction is residential HVAC (then possibly roofing) service businesses.
**Status of the niche:** **not validated.** This document proposes a 14-day test, not a conclusion.

---

## The honest starting position

What exists:
- A well-built portfolio with five real projects and honest, specific case studies.
- No organic traffic history, no analytics, no Search Console, no sitemap, no canonicals, and a dead custom domain.
- No HVAC client, no HVAC case study, no HVAC page, no offer packaged for a contractor.

What that means: **the website cannot currently generate inbound enquiries, and fixing SEO will not change that within 30 days.** SEO compounds over months. Income in weeks comes from outreach. The site's job in the next 30 days is to make outreach credible, not to be a traffic channel.

Treat the website as **sales collateral that happens to be public**.

---

## One segment, one offer, one cash-flow line

| | Choice | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Segment** | Residential HVAC companies, 2–20 people, US first (then UK) | Has real demand, real lead spend, and a workflow problem a solo developer can address |
| **Primary offer** | Lead-handling and follow-up implementation on the stack they already use | Avoids competing with ServiceTitan/Housecall Pro/Jobber; complements them |
| **Secondary (cash flow)** | Scoped website fixes: enquiry path, service pages, speed, measurement | Fast to deliver, adjacent evidence (tax-firm sites), lower trust barrier |
| **Deferred** | Full websites from scratch, SEO retainers, ad management, roofing | Slower to sell, harder to prove, or requires capabilities you have not demonstrated |

### What the research supports (and what it does not)

**Verified:** the product category is crowded and mature. ServiceTitan, Housecall Pro, Jobber and FieldEdge already sell lead pipelines, online requests, quote follow-up, reminders and review requests ([Housecall Pro HVAC CRM](https://www.housecallpro.com/resources/hvac-crm-software/), [Jobber HVAC](https://www.getjobber.com/industries/hvac/)). ServiceTitan sells campaign automation and call attribution ([Marketing Pro](https://marketplace.servicetitan.com/partner/servicetitan-marketing-pro)). CallRail and Podium sell call tracking and messaging. HVAC marketing agencies sell websites, SEO and ads ([Hook Agency](https://hookagency.com/hvac-marketing/)).

**Verified:** contractors discuss missed calls and lead quality openly, and the language is not uniform. One thread describes a caller ringing back within 15 minutes to check the business is still operating ([HVAC-Talk](https://www.hvac-talk.com/threads/missed-a-customer-call.2290105/)); another has a contractor saying they ignored 12 calls because they were booked eight days out ([HVAC-Talk](https://www.hvac-talk.com/threads/we-miss-a-call-all-the-times-problem.2291482/)).

**Not verified:** market share, typical marketing spend, who signs off on purchases, keyword volume, cost per lead. An agency guide suggests 7–10% of revenue for small-to-mid HVAC marketing ([Hook Agency](https://hookagency.com/blog/hvac-marketing-budget/)) — that is guidance, not measurement. Do not repeat it as a fact in outreach.

**Strategic consequence:** you cannot win by promising "more leads" or by replacing their software. You win by **making the seams work**: enquiry → their system, missed call → response, unsold estimate → follow-up, and a report the owner recognises.

## The offer, stated plainly

> **Lead-handling audit + fix for HVAC contractors.**
> I trace what happens to an enquiry from the moment it arrives to the moment a job is booked, find where it stalls, and fix the specific handoff — without replacing the software your office already uses.
> First step: a 20-minute call, then a short written summary of what I found.

Three deliverables, each sellable separately:
1. **Audit (paid, small):** trace calls, forms, after-hours, estimate follow-up; produce a one-page map of where enquiries go.
2. **Fix (scoped project):** implement the one or two changes that matter — website enquiry path, missed-call response, follow-up workflow, reporting.
3. **Website improvement (scoped):** service pages, contact path, mobile speed, measurement baseline.

Do not offer retainers until one project has been delivered and can be described honestly.

## The 14-day niche test

Do not commit to HVAC on faith. Run this and decide with evidence.

**Hypothesis:** residential HVAC owners with 2–20 staff will pay for a scoped lead-handling fix, sold through direct, personalised outreach.

**Test design (14 days):**

| Signal | Target | Reading |
|---|---|---|
| Researched prospects (name, city, site reviewed, one specific observation) | 40 | If you cannot find 40 with a visible problem, the segment is wrong |
| Personalised first contacts | 25 | Volume matters less than specificity |
| Replies | ≥ 3 | Below 3 means the message or the segment is off |
| Calls held | ≥ 2 | Interest without calls is not validation |
| Paid audit or scoped proposal accepted | **1** | This is the actual signal |

**Pass:** 1 paid engagement, or 2 qualified calls that both ask "how much and when can you start".
**Pivot:** ≥3 replies but no calls → the problem is real, the offer is unclear. Rewrite the offer, keep the segment.
**Fail:** <3 replies from 25 personalised contacts → change segment (try roofing, or general professional services), not the message alone.

Record every contact in the lead log. Do not count "opened" as a signal.

## Weekly operating rhythm (after day 30)

From the existing strategy document, adjusted for a solo operator:

- **10** researched prospects per week, each with a specific observation.
- **5** personalised first contacts per week.
- **2** follow-ups per contact, spaced 3 and 10 days.
- **2** partner conversations per month (designers, local marketers, IT providers who already serve contractors).
- **2** LinkedIn posts per month, each about a real build or decision.
- **1** article per fortnight, only when there is something true to say.

At 5 contacts/week and a realistic 10–20% reply rate, expect a handful of conversations per month. That is not a forecast — it is the arithmetic you should replace with your own numbers after 30 days.

## Risks and how to handle them

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| **Timezone**: UTC+5 vs US business hours | Offer two overlap windows (early US morning = your late afternoon). Say so on the page. |
| **Trust**: unknown developer, overseas | Lead with the demo, offer a small paid audit instead of a large project, use a written scope, take a deposit. |
| **Payment** | Confirm a working international method before the first call; put payment terms in the proposal. |
| **Scope creep** on a cheap audit | Fixed deliverable: one page, one recommendation, one call. |
| **Being treated as an agency** | You are not selling ads or rankings. Say what you do not do. |
| **Portfolio dilutes the offer** | Keep the homepage broad, make the HVAC page specific, and send outreach links only to the HVAC page. |
| **Niche is wrong** | The 14-day test exists to find that out cheaply. |

## What success looks like at day 30

| Metric | Target |
|---|---|
| Paid engagements | ≥ 1 |
| Qualified calls held | ≥ 2 |
| Personalised contacts sent | 25–40 |
| Reply rate | recorded, not assumed |
| Site: LCP mobile | ≤ 2.5 s |
| Site: indexed pages with canonicals | 10/10 |
| Site: enquiries traceable to a source | ≥ 1 |

If all seven are met, continue the niche and start the roofing question in month 2. If only the site metrics are met, the site is fine and the offer or segment is wrong — change the offer first, because it is cheaper to change than the segment.
